How I work

Four principles I have settled on after nine years of building and running distributed systems.

I design for failure

Things break. Brokers drop, jobs stall, nodes run out of memory. I build systems that degrade gracefully and make their failures visible, instead of quietly hoping they won’t.

I treat governance as the product

When many teams share one platform, contracts, access control, and observability aren’t overhead. They are the product. I would rather build that in from the start than bolt it on later.

I price cost into the design

Efficiency is a design constraint, not a quarterly cleanup. Cost, latency, and reliability are decisions you make up front, because that is when they are still cheap to change.

I keep novelty off the critical path

I reach for proven tools and keep novelty off the critical path. The interesting complexity belongs in the problem I am solving, not in the stack that has to hold up at 3 a.m.
